# Yellowstone Strategy Simulator

## Thesis

This project turns Trevor Allen's path to building Yellowstone Capital Advisors into an entrepreneurship strategy simulator. The main lesson is that founders do not simply choose between "take risk" and "avoid risk." They decide which risks are worth taking, which risks can be reduced, and how timing affects credibility, cash, hiring, control, and growth.

## How To Use It

Open `index.html` in a browser. The player launches a boutique advisory startup and makes eight founder decisions. The dashboard tracks six metrics at all times: Cash, Credibility, Team Capacity, Growth, Control, and Risk.

After each decision, the game gives feedback and a short business lesson. At the end, the player receives a founder archetype such as Disciplined Builder, Aggressive Scaler, Reputation-First Founder, or Calculated Risk-Taker.

## Presentation Opening

Our project is a browser-based decision-making game about entrepreneurial uncertainty. The player is not trying to find one perfect answer. Instead, each choice creates a tradeoff that connects to Trevor Allen's experience launching Yellowstone Capital Advisors and to class themes about founder motivation, business models, hiring, credibility, early wins, and control versus growth.

The simulation shows why Trevor's story is more nuanced than a simple "take the leap" founder narrative. His path shows the value of accumulating expertise before launching, reducing risk where failure would threaten the company, timing hiring with revenue, building credibility as a new entrant, and using early wins to overcome liability of newness.

## Scenario Map

Launch timing: Choose whether to launch immediately, build more industry experience first, or start part-time.

Business model: Choose between a proven success-fee model, a new pricing structure, or a hybrid approach.

First hire: Decide whether to hire before securing a client, wait until revenue is closer, or use contractors.

Credibility: Decide how to build trust as a new entrant competing against older firms.

Deal volume: Choose highly winnable deals, chase rapid growth, or create a strict screening process.

Solo founder pressure: Decide how to manage without a co-founder or large founding team.

First major client: Choose how to turn the first deal into momentum.

Scale decision: Decide whether to stay lean and controlled, scale faster, or scale through a narrow niche.

## Concept Summary

Risk reduction vs. innovation: The game shows that using a proven model can be a smart entrepreneurial choice when the rest of the venture is already uncertain.

Control vs. growth: Faster scaling can raise growth but reduce control and increase risk.

Timing hiring with revenue: Hiring too early can create credibility, but it also creates cash pressure before revenue is guaranteed.

Liability of newness: New firms must prove trust through specialization, relationships, and early results.

Evolving founder motivation: Trevor's earlier ventures and corporate experience were not wasted; they became part of the resource base for Yellowstone.
